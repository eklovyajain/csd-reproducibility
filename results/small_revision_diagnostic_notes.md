# Small-Revision Boundary Diagnostic (internal)

Data: deidentified N = 451, all-mpnet-base-v2, canonical `csd_decompose`. All 451 statuses `ok`.
Generated by `small_revision_diagnostic.py`. Supporting files:
`results/revision_magnitude_summary.csv`, `results/small_revision_diagnostic.csv`,
`results/revision_magnitude_orientation.csv`. The qualitative flags are one reader's judgments
of the 20 smallest-M cases, not computed quantities; they do not define a cutoff.

**A. Are there observations where M is so small that normalized orientation appears substantively meaningless?**
No case among the 20 smallest was judged meaningless (`no`: 0 of 20). The smallest case
(row 265, M = 0.093, cos_IF = 0.996) is a near-copy whose direction rests on one added phrase;
it is flagged `caution`, not `no`, because that phrase echoes the critique. Four other cases are
`caution` because the final text is mostly a paraphrase, so rewording may dominate F − I.
The minimum M is above zero (0.093), so no observation hits the F = I undefined case.

**B. Do the smallest-M cases look like genuine revisions or essentially no change?**
Mostly genuine revisions: 1 `minimal`, 12 `small_but_real`, 7 `clear_revision`. Five of the
20 smallest-M cases reverse their channel decision (rows 194, 256, 82, 26, 270). Small M under
MPNet reflects shared topic vocabulary across initial and final texts. It is not a reliable
marker of "no substantive change".

**C. Does normalized alignment appear systematically distorted among very small-M observations?**
Not toward extremes. Bottom-5% M (n = 23, M ≤ 0.489) has aligned_normalized mean 0.264
(SD 0.116, range 0.027–0.505), compared with 0.424 (SD 0.143, range 0.042–0.876) for the remaining
95% (n = 428). Small-M values fall inside the bulk of the overall distribution. However, M is
moderately correlated with orientation across the full sample: aligned_normalized r = .49,
ρ = .49; residual_normalized r = −.46, ρ = −.49. So normalized orientation is not independent
of revision size. This diagnostic does not establish whether that reflects behavior (larger
revisions engaging the critique more) or geometry. These numbers are descriptive only, with no test.

**D. Is there evidence that we need an exclusion threshold now?**
No. No case was judged uninterpretable, and the low-M group is not extreme. The low-M tail also
contains clear revisions, including decision reversals, so an M cutoff would remove genuine
revisions. The M–orientation correlation is a reporting and interpretation issue, not grounds
for exclusion.

**E. Recommended appendix language**
The preferred language is supported as written:

> "CSD orientation is undefined when F = I and should be interpreted cautiously when revision
> magnitude is very small. In the present sample, observed revision magnitudes remained above
> zero; sensitivity checks of the smallest-magnitude revisions were used to assess whether
> normalized orientation was being driven by negligible semantic change."

Optional addition, if the appendix should report what the check found:

> "Among the smallest-magnitude revisions, most involved substantive changes in reasoning,
> including several reversals of the recommended decision, and normalized alignment in this group
> was lower rather than more extreme. Because normalized alignment correlates moderately with
> revision magnitude (r ≈ .49), we report revision magnitude alongside orientation."
